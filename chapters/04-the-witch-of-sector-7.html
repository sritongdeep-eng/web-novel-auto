    CHAPTER 4 — The Witch of Sector 7

    Vera’s hideout was a fortress of blood-tech and old-world salvage. She moved through its corridors with the confidence of someone who’d defended this place for years, her cybernetic arm humming with stored energy.

    Kael followed, his own arm still glowing from the Veridian connection. The threads had dimmed slightly, but they were there — a permanent reminder that he was no longer entirely human.

    “You need training,” Vera said, leading him into a training chamber. The room was lined with holographic projections of pre-Collapse interfaces — floating keyboards, data-streams, security protocols.

    “I can already interface with machines,” Kael said.

    “You can touch them,” Vera corrected. “There’s a difference. A Null Conduit who doesn’t learn control is a weapon that could detonate the entire grid. I’m going to teach you to read the static.”

    She placed a bio-cable in his hand. “Connect to me.”

    Kael hesitated. “Through your arm?”

    “Through my core. It’s safe. I designed it.”

    He pressed the needle into his forearm and connected the other end to the port at Vera’s wrist. Her cybernetic casing opened, revealing the blood-tech core beneath — a cluster of crimson filaments that pulsed with light.

    Connection established.

    The world dissolved into static, then reformed as a vast library. Shelves stretched to infinity, each holding a fragment of data — a memory, a skill, a piece of knowledge from the old world.

    “Welcome to the Archive,” Vera’s voice echoed. “This is where we store everything the corporations tried to destroy. Every book, every song, every scientific paper. The old world didn’t just leave us machines. It left us its mind.”

    Kael walked the aisles. He saw a physics textbook floating in a data-sphere. A jazz recording, the notes visible as colored waves. A child’s drawing of a dragon, preserved in perfect clarity.

    “This is what Veridian protects,” Vera said. “Not just the ability to restore Earth, but the memory of what Earth was. Without this, humanity would rebuild as slaves — strong, efficient, but hollow.”

    She led him to a section labeled “VERIDIAN FRAGMENTS.”

    “These are the other pieces of the core,” she said. “There are nine more like Veridian-0, scattered across the wastelands. We need to find them all and bring them here.”

    Kael touched a fragment. It pulsed under his fingers.

    “How do I find them?” he asked.

    “The blood-threads in your arm act as a compass,” Vera said. “When you’re near a fragment, they’ll pull you toward it. But be careful — Arasaka has scanners that can detect Null Conduit signatures from kilometers away.”

    A alarm blared. Red lights flashed.

    “They found us,” Vera said calmly. “Again.”

    She disconnected the cable and grabbed her coat. “We have a safe house in Sector 12. Move.”

    They ran through the parking structure’s corridors, Arasaka troopers hot on their heels. Vera stopped at a maintenance hatch, placed her palm on the lock, and let her cybernetic core pulse once.

    The hatch opened.

    They descended into the undercity tunnels, the sounds of pursuit fading above them. Kael’s arm throbbed — not with pain, but with purpose.

    He was no longer just a data-runner. He was a Null Conduit. A key to something ancient and powerful.

    And for the first time in his life, he had a mission bigger than survival.
