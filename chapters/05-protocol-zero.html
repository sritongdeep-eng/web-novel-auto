    CHAPTER 5 — Protocol Zero

    Arasaka didn’t send troopers. They sent a Protocol Zero unit — a corporate assassin enhanced with blood-tech implants that made them faster, stronger, and able to track Null Conduits through the static itself.

    Kael and Vera barely escaped Sector 9, their safe house reduced to ash by the time they reached the tunnels. The assassin’s crimson eyes glowed in the smoke, watching them flee.

    “It’s a tracker,” Vera said, checking her scanners. “They’ve tagged you with a blood-beacon. Every corporate unit within fifty kilometers can see your signature.”

    “Can you remove it?” Kael asked.

    “Not without killing you. But I can mask it — temporarily.”

    They stopped in a hidden chamber beneath the old subway system. Vera set up a blood-tech jammer — a jury-rigged device made from salvaged server parts and her own core energy. The crimson threads in Kael’s arm dimmed as the jammer activated.

    “This buys us three days,” Vera said, exhausted. “After that, the beacon reasserts. We need to find the next Veridian fragment before then.”

    Kael closed his eyes. He focused on the threads in his arm, letting them expand his awareness beyond his body. He felt the city around him — the blood-tech grid thrumming through every wall, every street, every living person. And somewhere in the distance, a faint pulse. A fragment.

    “I can feel it,” he said. “North. Near the old coast.”

    Vera nodded. “The coastal data centers. Arasaka has been excavating them for years. They must have found another fragment.”

    “Then we’re too late.”

    “Or just in time.”

    The coastal ruins stretched for kilometers — skeletal remains of pre-Collapse server farms half-buried in sand and salt. Arasaka had fortified the area with blood-sensors and automated turrets, but Vera knew a back entrance through the drainage tunnels.

    They emerged into a cavernous server room, the air thick with rust and sea spray. In the center, on a pedestal of ancient stone, sat a server that glowed with the same crimson light as Veridian-0.

    But something was wrong.

    The server pulsed erratically, its light flickering like a dying heartbeat. Around it, corporate technicians worked feverishly, attaching bio-cables and extraction devices.

    “They’re siphoning it,” Vera hissed. “If they drain the fragment’s energy, Veridian will never awaken.”

    Kael stepped forward before Vera could stop him.

    “Hey!” he called out.

    The technicians turned. Their eyes were dead — enhanced with corporate implants that left no room for independent thought. One of them raised a blood-rifle.

    Kael didn’t think. He reached out with his threads, feeling the server’s pulse, and pulled.

    The fragment reacted. Its crimson light surged, flowing from the server into Kael’s arm, through his body, and out his other hand in a wave of pure data. The technicians screamed as their implants overloaded, their bodies convulsing from the feedback.

    The corporate commander in the room — a woman in an Arasaka uniform — drew a blood-blade and charged.

    Vera intercepted her, their cybernetic cores clashing in a shower of crimson sparks. The commander was strong, but Vera had something she didn’t: eighty years of blood-tech mastery.

    Kael finished channeling the fragment’s energy. He felt it merge with Veridian-0 in his mind — a piece of a puzzle clicking into place. The threads in his arm now glowed brighter, more complex.

    The commander fell. Vera stood over her, her core pulsing.

    “Tell Arasaka,” Vera said, “that the Null Conduit is coming for them.”

    They escaped with the fragment cradled in a bio-cache Vera had brought. Behind them, the coastal data center collapsed — not from explosives, but from the fragment’s final act of defiance, overloading every corporate system in a fifty-kilometer radius.

    Kael carried the cache like a holy relic. Nine more fragments to find.

    Nine more steps toward Veridian.
