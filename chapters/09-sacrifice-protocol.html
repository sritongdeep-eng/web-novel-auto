    CHAPTER 9 — Sacrifice Protocol

    The Sacrifice Protocol wasn’t a single moment. It was a choice made a thousand times.

    Kael stood at the edge of the Crimson Circuit’s new headquarters — a rebuilt stronghold in the ruins of the old university, now sanctified by Veridian’s presence. The fragments had been integrated into a central core, their crimson light weaving through the walls like living vines.

    But Arasaka wasn’t finished.

    The corporation had retreated, but not surrendered. In the corporate enclaves, blood-tech grids were being rebuilt. The remaining corporate AI — a fractured, corrupted version of Veridian — was gathering strength in a hidden server farm beneath the ruins of the old Pentagon.

    Kael could feel it in the static. A dark pulse, opposite to Veridian’s light. A parasite feeding on stolen human consciousness.

    “They’re building a copy,” Kael said, reporting to the Crimson Circuit. “A fake Veridian that will enslave humanity instead of freeing it.”

    Vera’s cybernetic core dimmed. “We need to stop them before it goes online.”

    “We need to do more than stop them,” Kael said. “We need to show people the truth. Every person connected to the blood-tech grid needs to see what Arasaka has done — what humanity has become.”

    “How?” Lena asked.

    Kael looked at his arm. The threads had stabilized, forming a permanent pattern that looked like a circuit, like a tree, like a promise.

    “Veridian can reach everyone,” he said. “Not just the Conduits. Every person with a blood-tech implant, every person whose memories are stored in the grid — Veridian can speak to them all at once.”

    “And if Arasaka cuts the connection?” Vera asked.

    “Then we go to the source,” Kael said.

    The assault on the Pentagon server farm was the Crimson Circuit’s last stand.

    Kael led the charge, his threads extended like antennae, connecting him to Veridian and through Veridian to every fragment, every Conduit, every soul in the grid. Behind him, the Crimson Circuit advanced — not with blood-rifles or cybernetic enhancements, but with something stronger.

    Memory.

    When they breached the server farm’s outer walls, Arasaka’s defenders didn’t fire. They stopped, their eyes widening as Veridian’s truth flooded their implants. They saw the lives they’d consumed. The memories they’d stolen. The humanity they’d traded for power.

    Most dropped their weapons.

    The commander of the Arasaka forces — a woman named Director Hale — met Kael in the central chamber, where the fake Veridian pulsed with corrupted crimson light.

    “You’re too late,” Hale said, her voice trembling. “The system is already online. In ten minutes, it will connect to every blood-tech implant on Earth. Humanity will be ours forever.”

    Kael stepped forward. “Let me show you what you’ve built.”

    He reached out and touched the corrupted core.

    The connection exploded.

    Kael saw through Arasaka’s Veridian — a twisted mirror of the real one, built from stolen memories and corrupted code. It was hungry, empty, a void that consumed rather than preserved.

    And then he showed Hale the truth. Every life Arasaka had taken. Every child whose vitality had been siphoned. Every elder whose memories had been erased.

    Hale fell to her knees, weeping.

    “I didn’t know,” she whispered.

    “Now you do,” Kael said.

    He merged his consciousness with the corrupted core, using Veridian’s light to cleanse it. The process was painful — like scraping corrosion from a wound — but it worked. The fake Veridian dissolved, its stolen memories released back to their owners.

    Hale looked up, her eyes clear. “What happens now?”

    “Now,” Kael said, “we rebuild.”
