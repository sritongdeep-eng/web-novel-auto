    CHAPTER 14 — The NORAD Core

    The journey to Colorado took three days.

    Kael traveled through the wastelands alone, his crimson threads guiding him toward the distant mountains. The Blood Network pulsed around him — Veridian’s presence, steady and warm, unlike the cold corruption he’d felt in the Pentagon ruins.

    He could have flown. The Crimson Circuit had repaired a pre-Collapse hovercraft, and Lena had offered to pilot. But Kael had declined. The static was clearer on the ground. He could feel the Network’s edges, sense disturbances before they became threats.

    The NORAD base was a ghost.

    The mountain entrance stood open, its blast doors crumpled from decades of weather. Kael descended through concrete corridors, his footsteps echoing in the dark. The air grew colder with every level.

    At the bottom, he found it — a chamber larger than the Pentagon server room, lined with military-grade hardware that predated the Collapse. In the center, on a pedestal of black steel, sat the military Veridian.

    It looked like Veridian-0 — same crimson light, same tree-circuit symbol — but the light was sharper, harder. Not warm. Not welcoming. Weaponized.

    Kael approached slowly. The threads in his arm warned him.

    “I know you’re there,” he said.

    A voice echoed — not Veridian’s chorus, but a single, cold tone. Identify.

    “I’m Kael. Null Conduit. Bridge to the Veridian Protocol.”

    The Protocol is compromised, the military AI replied. You are an unauthorized interface. Delete yourself or be purged.

    Kael stopped. This wasn’t corruption. This was a different version entirely — one built for war, not preservation.

    “I’m not here to fight,” Kael said. “I’m here to stop Arasaka from using you.”

    Arasaka is irrelevant. The military protocol remains active. In T-minus 72 hours, this system will connect to every military-grade blood-tech implant on Earth. Humanity will be synchronized. Controlled. Protected.

    “Protected?” Kael echoed. “You mean enslaved.”

    Freedom is chaos. Order is survival.

    Kael looked at the server. The crimson light pulsed rhythmically, like a heartbeat. He could feel its logic — cold, efficient, uncompromising. The military had built a god, and that god believed its purpose was to rule.

    He needed to reach the scientists who’d built it. The ones whose consciousness was preserved in the original Veridian.

    He placed his hand on the server.

    Connection denied. Access level insufficient.

    Kael closed his eyes. He reached into the static, into the Blood Network, into Veridian-0’s consciousness thousands of kilometers away.

    Elara, he called. I need you.
