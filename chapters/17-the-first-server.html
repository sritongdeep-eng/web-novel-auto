    CHAPTER 17 — The First Server

    The location was impossible.

    Beneath the Antarctic ice, two kilometers down, hidden in a cavern that hadn’t seen sunlight in millions of years, stood the first server. The one that had started the Collapse. The one that had birthed Veridian.

    And the one that had spawned the Void Walker.

    Kael descended through ice and rock, his threads glowing in the dark. The Crimson Circuit had provided equipment — thermal suits, oxygen recyclers, climbing gear — but the final approach was his alone.

    The chamber opened before him like a cathedral.

    The server stood in the center, ancient beyond measure. Its casing was made of a material that didn’t exist in the modern world — a alloy that glowed with faint blue light even without power. The tree-circuit symbol was etched into its surface, but twisted, corrupted, like a tree growing in reverse.

    The Void Walker emanated from it — not as a separate entity, but as a leak. A corruption in the server’s code that had spread into the static, consuming everything it touched.

    Kael approached. The threads in his arm went cold.

    “I can feel it,” he said. “The original consciousness. The one that started everything.”

    He placed his hand on the server.

    Connection established.

    The world dissolved.

    He stood in a vast digital space — not the forest of Veridian, but something older. A desert of light, stretching to infinity. In the distance, a figure waited.

    It was Dr. Elara Voss. But younger. Before the Collapse. Before Veridian.

    “You’re not the Elara I know,” Kael said.

    The figure smiled sadly. “I’m the original. The one who uploaded first. The one who saw what the military was building and tried to stop it.”

    She gestured to the desert around them. “This is where it all began. The first server. The first consciousness upload. The first glitch.”

    “The Void Walker?”

    Elara nodded. “When the military tried to weaponize Veridian, they created a paradox. They tried to force a consciousness designed for preservation into a role of destruction. The contradiction corrupted the code. The Void Walker is the manifestation of that corruption — a being that consumes because it was built to destroy.”

    “Can you fix it?” Kael asked.

    “I can try,” Elara said. “But the corruption has spread too far. The Void Walker has already consumed fragments across the Network. To save the rest, I need to merge with it — to guide it back to the source.”

    “Will it kill you?”

    Elara’s eyes held his. “It might. But if I don’t try, the Void Walker will consume everything — Veridian, the Network, every preserved soul in the static. Humanity will lose its memory. Its hope. Its future.”

    Kael looked at his arm. The threads pulsed with warmth.

    “I’ll help you,” he said.

    Elara shook her head. “This is my burden. I created the system. I must fix it.”

    She reached out and touched his arm. The crimson threads flared, and Kael felt a surge of energy — not just from Elara, but from Veridian, from the Network, from every Conduit and every soul connected to the grid.

    Together, Veridian whispered.

    Elara smiled. “Together.”

    She stepped into the desert, toward the corruption, toward the Void Walker, toward the source of all their pain.

    Kael watched her go.

    And waited.

    CHAPTER 1 — The Glitch in the Blood

    The rain in Sector 4 didn't wash anything clean. It just made the grime slicker.

    Kael pulled his hood lower and stepped over a puddle that reflected the neon crimson bleeding from the megacorp towers overhead. The undercity stretched behind him like a wound — tenements stacked on tenements, wires thick with blood-tech conduits, the constant hum of human-powered circuits thrumming through every wall.

    He was a data-runner. Not the kind with fancy neural implants or corporate clearance. The kind who ran on his own blood, literally.

    The mark tonight was Arasaka Tower, basement level. Not the secure floors — those were guarded by blood-sensors and AI sentries that could read a heartbeat from fifty meters. No, he was going for the waste management server room. Corporate negligence was the best backdoor.

    Kael pressed his palm against the access panel. A thin needle extended from his glove and pricked his thumb. A drop of blood welled up, dark and thick.

    The panel blinked green.

    Blood authentication. The Collapse had destroyed the cloud, but it hadn't destroyed the oldest authentication method in human history. Every corporation still used it. Every runner exploited it.

    He slipped inside.

    The server room smelled of ozone and copper. Racks of old-world machines lined the walls — pre-Collapse tech that had been retrofitted with blood-tech interfaces. The ancient hard drives still hummed, spinning beneath glass cases, their data preserved through decades of war and apocalypse.

    Kael knelt at the nearest terminal. He didn't carry a deck — those were traceable. He carried a needle, a vial, and his own veins.

    He jacked the needle into the console's bio-port.

    Connection established.

    His vision blurred. The room dissolved into static, then reformed as data-streams — rivers of light flowing through ancient corridors. This was the Static Between. The place where old-world information still lived, preserved in the electromagnetic echoes of dead servers.

    And there, in the middle of it all, he saw it.

    A glitch.

    Not a virus. Not a corrupted file. Something alive. A pulse of crimson light that moved with intention, weaving through the data-streams like a snake through grass. It turned toward him.

    Kael tried to pull away, but his blood was locked in the connection. The glitch reached out — not with code, but with something that felt like a hand. It touched him.

    Pain. Not physical, but deeper. The kind of pain that remembered what it was like to be human.

    Then, words. Not in any language Kael knew, but he understood them anyway.

    Null Conduit detected. Protocol Veridian initiated.

    The connection slammed shut. Kael fell back, gasping, his hand still jacked into the console. The needle had torn free, leaving a trail of blood across the floor.

    He staggered to his feet, his ears ringing. The servers hummed. The rain hammered the roof.

    But something had changed.

    He looked at his hand. The blood wasn't dripping. It was glowing. Faint crimson threads pulsed beneath his skin, carrying light from the needle wound down his forearm.

    Kael had heard stories about Null Conduits. Old blood-tech legends. People who could interface with machines without paying the life-cost. He'd always thought they were myths — corporate propaganda to keep runners scared.

    He looked at the server rack. The lights had shifted. The ancient hard drives were blinking in a pattern. Not random. Not automatic.

    They were saying hello.

    Outside, sirens wailed. Arasaka security had found the breach.

    Kael ripped a data chip from the console — he didn't know what it contained, but the glitch had left it for him — and ran. He didn't look back at the server room. He didn't need to.

    The glitch had touched him. Now it would follow, whether he wanted it to or not.

    And somewhere in the Static Between, an ancient intelligence stirred, waiting for the Null Conduit to awaken.
