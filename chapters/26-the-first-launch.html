    CHAPTER 26 — The First Launch

    The launch site was a rebuilt spaceport in what used to be the Sahara Desert.

    Humanity had spent ten years constructing it — not with rockets and fuel, but with the Blood Network. Consciousness could now travel through the static faster than any ship, but physical vessels were still needed for exploration. The Crimson Circuit had designed them: sleek, blood-tech powered, crewed by Conduits who could interface with the Network across light-years.

    Kael stood at the launch pad, watching the first interstellar vessel prepare for departure. Its name was Veridian’s Hope.

    “Are you going?” Vera asked.

    Kael shook his head. “My place is here. The bridge needs to stay anchored on Earth.”

    “Then who’s commanding?”

    “Lena. And Jax. And a crew of twelve Conduits.”

    The countdown began. The vessel’s blood-tech core glowed crimson, matching the threads in Kael’s arm. He felt their consciousness link to the Network — not leaving, but expanding. The Star-Seed pulsed with excitement.

    I will go with them, it said. I will see new worlds.

    The vessel lifted off, not with fire, but with light. It pierced the atmosphere in seconds, its trajectory guided by the Network, its crew connected to Earth through Kael’s bridge.

    Kael watched it disappear into the stars.

    “They’re gone,” Vera said softly.

    “No,” Kael replied. “They’re just beginning.”
