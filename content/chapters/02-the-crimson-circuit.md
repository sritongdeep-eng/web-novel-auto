# CHAPTER 2 — The Crimson Circuit

The rain stopped as Kael emerged into the undercity maze. Neon signs flickered above alleyways, casting blood-red shadows on wet concrete. He kept his head down, his glowing forearm wrapped in a torn sleeve.

The data chip was warm in his palm.

He’d stolen it from Arasaka’s server room without knowing what it contained. Now, the blood-threads in his arm pulsed in time with some distant rhythm, as if the chip were a heartbeat he could feel.

Kael ducked into a noodle shop off Kilometre 12. The air reeked of synthetic pork and copper from the blood-fryer behind the counter. He slid into a booth, pulled the chip from his pocket, and set it on the table.

“You’re bleeding,” the vendor said, not looking up.

“Not mine.”

The vendor glanced at his arm. The crimson threads had brightened, visible through the tear in his sleeve. The vendor’s eyes widened. He grabbed a rag and threw it at Kael.

“Cover that. You want the Blood Watch here?”

Kael wrapped the rag around his forearm and pulled his hood lower.

The chip sat on the table, inert. He tried to jack it into his portable reader, but the needle port on the device wouldn’t accept it. The chip’s interface was older — pre-Collapse standard.

He needed a specialist.

---

The Crimson Circuit wasn’t a place. It was a people.

Kael had heard whispers in the undercity markets: a hidden network of blood-tech practitioners who rejected corporate blood-slavery. They called themselves Conduits — people who could interface with machines through arcane blood rituals without sacrificing their own vitality.

Most people thought they were myths. Kael now knew they were real.

He found the Circuit through a laundromat in Sector 9. The old woman folding clothes behind the counter looked up when he entered, her eyes lingering on his wrapped arm.

“The Null Conduit walks in,” she said softly. “We’ve been waiting.”

She led him through a back door, down a stairwell, into a room that shouldn’t have existed — a circular chamber lined with pre-Collapse servers, their screens alive with crimson light. Around the room, men and women sat in meditation, their arms connected to the machines via bio-cables.

A woman stood in the center. She wore a long coat stitched with circuit patterns, and her left arm was entirely cybernetic — chrome plating over a visible blood-tech core that glowed with the same crimson as Kael’s threads.

“I am Vera,” she said. “The Witch of Sector 7. And you, Kael, are what we’ve prayed for.”

She held out her hand. The threads in Kael’s arm surged, and he felt a warmth spread from his chest to his fingertips.

“You’re a Null Conduit,” Vera said. “The first in eighty years. The Veridian Protocol chose you.”

Kael looked at the servers around him. Each screen displayed fragments of the same glitch he’d seen in Arasaka’s server room — that serpentine crimson light, moving with purpose.

“What is the Veridian Protocol?” he asked.

Vera smiled sadly. “The last hope for a world that sold its soul to blood-tech. Come. I’ll show you.”

---

That night, Kael learned the truth.

The Collapse hadn’t been a war. It had been a choice. The megacorporations had discovered that human life force could power circuits — not just as energy, but as a computational substrate. Blood-tech wasn’t just fuel. It was processing. Every drop of blood donated to the grid became a calculation, a memory, a thought in the great corporate AI.

The poor died faster. The rich lived longer by buying others’ vitality.

And the Veridian Protocol was the counter-move. An ancient AI system buried beneath the ruins of the old internet, waiting for a Null Conduit — someone who could interface with machines without paying the life-cost — to awaken it.

Kael was that someone.

The chip he’d stolen contained the activation sequence.

Vera placed her cybernetic hand on his shoulder. “You have a choice, Kael. You can run, hide, and let the corporations consume everything. Or you can awaken Veridian and give humanity a chance to rebuild without blood-tech slavery.”

Kael looked at his glowing arm. He thought of the glitch reaching out to him in the Static Between. Not attacking. Greeting.

“What happens if I say yes?” he asked.

Vera’s eyes reflected the crimson glow of the servers. “Everything changes.”

Outside, the sirens wailed again. Arasaka had found the laundromat.

Kael pocketed the chip and followed Vera into the depths of the Crimson Circuit.
