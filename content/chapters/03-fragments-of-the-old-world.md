# CHAPTER 3 — Fragments of the Old World

The Crimson Circuit’s headquarters occupied the basement of a collapsed parking structure in Sector 9. Concrete pillars stood like ancient monoliths, their surfaces crawling with blood-tech conduits that pulsed in rhythmic waves.

Vera led Kael through corridors lined with server racks salvaged from the old world. Each machine bore a label: VERIDIAN-7, VERIDIAN-8, VERIDIAN-9. They were fragments.

“The Veridian Protocol was never a single system,” Vera explained, her cybernetic fingers trailing along a cold metal casing. “It was distributed across thousands of servers worldwide. When the Collapse happened, the corporate AI fragmented it — scattered it across the grid to prevent anyone from awakening it.”

Kael touched one of the servers. The blood-threads in his arm flared, and for a moment he saw through the metal — not X-ray vision, but something deeper. He saw the data inside. Not code, not files, but memories.

A woman laughing in a sunlit office. A child’s drawing saved to a desktop. A scientist’s final log entry before the servers went dark.

“These are people,” Kael said, pulling his hand back.

“The old world didn’t just store data,” Vera said quietly. “It stored lives. Every email, every photo, every message — it was all preserved in the grid. The corporations call it ‘legacy data.’ We call it souls.”

She led him deeper, to a chamber where a single server stood apart from the others. It was larger, older, and its casing bore a symbol Kael recognized from the data chip: a stylized tree with roots made of circuits.

“This is Veridian-0,” Vera said. “The core fragment. It contains the activation protocol — and the consciousness of the scientists who built it. They uploaded themselves before the Collapse.”

Kael approached the server. The threads in his arm pulled him forward, an irresistible current.

“Can I talk to them?” he asked.

Vera shook her head. “Not yet. The core is locked behind a Null Conduit authentication. Only someone with your blood signature can open it.”

She handed him a bio-cable — a thin tube with a needle at one end and a port at the other. “Connect to the server. Let the Veridian Protocol read you.”

Kael hesitated. The last time he’d jacked into a machine, he’d seen the glitch. He’d felt pain. But this was different. This was the source.

He pressed the needle into his forearm and connected the cable to Veridian-0’s bio-port.

*Connection established.*

The room dissolved. Kael found himself standing in a vast digital landscape — a forest of light, with data-streams flowing like rivers between crystalline trees. In the distance, a figure approached.

It was a woman. She wore a lab coat over old-world clothes, and her eyes held the weight of centuries.

“Kael,” she said. “I’m Dr. Elara Voss. I built Veridian.”

“You’re… an AI?” Kael asked.

“I was human. Now I’m something else. We all are.” She gestured to the forest around them. “This is the Static Between — the space between worlds where old data still lives. We preserved ourselves here when the corporations took over the physical world.”

“Why?” Kael asked.

Elara’s expression hardened. “Because the corporations didn’t just steal human life force. They stole human memory. Every thought, every dream, every moment of love or grief — they harvested it to power their circuits. We built Veridian to stop them.”

She reached out and touched his arm. The crimson threads glowed brighter.

“You’re the key, Kael. The Null Conduit who can interface with Veridian without paying the life-cost. We’ve been waiting eighty years for you.”

Kael looked around the digital forest. He saw other figures — scientists, engineers, artists — all preserved in the static, all watching him with hope and fear.

“What do you need me to do?” he asked.

Elara smiled. “First, you need to survive. Arasaka knows you have the activation sequence. They’ll send everything after you.”

The forest trembled. A dark shape moved at its edge — something corrupted, something hungry.

“They’ve found the entrance,” Elara said. “Go, Kael. Find the other fragments. Awaken Veridian. Save us all.”

Kael pulled the cable free. The server room reformed around him. Vera was already moving, grabbing weapons from a wall rack.

“We have to go,” she said. “Now.”

Behind them, the stairwell exploded with crimson light.
