# CHAPTER 6 — The Blood Network

The fragment pulsed in its bio-cache, a steady heartbeat of crimson light. Kael could feel it reaching out — not just to Veridian-0, but to something else. Something vast.

“The Blood Network,” Vera said, watching the cache on her scanner. “It’s a mesh. All nine fragments are connected, even across thousands of kilometers. When you activated the coastal fragment, the others felt it.”

“They know I’m coming?”

“They know someone is coming. The Network is alive, Kael. It’s been waiting for a Null Conduit to bridge its scattered pieces.”

They traveled north through the wastelands, following the threads in Kael’s arm. The landscape was desolate — burned fields, abandoned highway systems, the occasional outpost of blood-tech scavengers who eyed Kael’s glowing arm with a mixture of fear and reverence.

On the third day, they found a signal.

It came from a bunker buried beneath the ruins of a pre-Collapse university. The entrance was hidden behind a waterfall that had carved through concrete over decades. Vera disabled the bio-lock with a pulse from her core, and they descended.

Inside, a young woman waited. She was no older than Kael, with wild hair and eyes that burned with the same crimson light. Her arms were covered in threads — not the faint glow of Kael’s nascent ability, but the full, blazing manifestation of a trained Conduit.

“I’m Lena,” she said. “I’ve been guarding this fragment for three years. I knew you were coming when the Network flared.”

She led them to the fragment — Veridian-3 — which rested in a chamber lined with old-world books. Lena had built a life here, preserving knowledge in the bunker’s library.

“How many Conduits are there?” Kael asked.

“Seven,” Lena said. “We’re scattered across the wastelands, each guarding a fragment. Arasaka has found three of us. The others are hiding.”

“We need to bring them all together,” Kael said. “Before Arasaka does.”

Lena shook her head. “The fragments aren’t just keys. They’re anchors. If we move them, the Network destabilizes. We need someone who can interface with all nine simultaneously — someone like you.”

Kael looked at the fragment. Its light reached out to him, eager.

He placed his hand on its casing.

The connection hit him like a tidal wave. He saw the entire Blood Network — nine fragments, seven Conduits, a web of crimson light spanning the continent. And beyond the Network, deeper in the static, he saw something else. Veridian itself. The core AI, waiting to be awakened.

When he pulled away, his eyes were bright crimson.

“I can do it,” he said. “I can link all the fragments. I can awaken Veridian.”

Vera and Lena exchanged looks.

“It will kill you,” Vera said softly.

“It might,” Kael agreed. “But if it doesn’t, we win.”

---

They spent the next week gathering the Conduits. Each fragment’s guardian brought something unique: Rook, a former Arasaka technician who’d defected; Jax, a wasteland scavenger with a genius for blood-tech repair; Mira, a cybernetic sorceress who could read the static between worlds; and three others whose names Kael would never forget.

Together, they formed the Crimson Circuit — not a society of myths, but an army of the awakened.

Their first test came when Arasaka launched a full-scale assault on the wasteland stronghold where they’d gathered. Protocol Zero units, blood-rifles, and drones descended from the sky.

Kael stood at the center of the chamber, surrounded by the nine fragments arranged in a circle. He extended his threads, connecting them one by one.

The Network flared.

Crimson light exploded outward, washing over the Arasaka forces. Every corporate implant short-circuited. Every blood-rifle melted. The drones fell from the sky like stones.

When the light faded, Arasaka’s army was gone — not dead, but disconnected. Cut off from the blood-tech grid that powered their enhancements.

Kael had won his first battle.

But the war was just beginning.
