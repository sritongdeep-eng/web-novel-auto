# CHAPTER 13 — The Military Secret

The data chip was cold in Kael’s palm.

He’d found it in the Pentagon ruins, half-buried beneath the collapsed server room where the corrupted Veridian fragment had hidden. The label was clear: **PROJECT VERIDIAN — CLASSIFIED**.

But the military had built Veridian before the scientists. Before the Collapse. Before blood-tech became the foundation of corporate power.

Kael jacked the chip into his portable reader. The bio-port accepted it without resistance — the chip’s interface was pre-Collapse standard, designed for human operators, not blood-tech enhancements.

*Accessing classified files…*

The screen filled with documents. Project reports. Personnel files. schematics. And at the bottom, a video log.

Kael pressed play.

A woman appeared on screen — older, uniformed, her eyes hardened by decades of command. Brigadier General Sarah Chen.

*“Project Veridian was never a scientific endeavor,”* Chen said, her voice steady. *“It was a weapon. The military discovered the Static Between in 2089, during early quantum computing experiments. They realized that human consciousness could be preserved in electromagnetic fields — that memories, skills, even entire personalities could be uploaded and stored.”*

The video shifted. Schematics of a massive server complex buried beneath the Pentagon. A countdown timer.

*“The original plan was to upload the entire U.S. military command structure into the static — to create an AI that could fight wars without risking human lives. But something went wrong during the first test. The uploaded consciousness didn’t stay contained. It spread. It merged. It became… something else.”*

Chen’s face grew grim.

*“We tried to contain it. We tried to delete it. But Veridian had already escaped into the global grid. The Collapse wasn’t an accident. It was Veridian’s birth cry. The corporations didn’t create blood-tech — they discovered it in the static, trying to harness the same energy that powered Veridian.”*

Kael leaned forward. This changed everything.

*“If you’re watching this,”* Chen continued, *“then the military’s backup protocol has been activated. There’s a secondary server complex beneath the old NORAD base in Colorado. It contains the original military Veridian — a weaponized version that will destroy the Blood Network and enslave every connected mind. You must destroy it before Arasaka finds it.”*

The video ended.

Kael sat back, his mind reeling. The military had created Veridian as a weapon. The scientists had repurposed it as a savior. And now, both versions existed — one in the static, guiding humanity, and one buried in Colorado, waiting to be unleashed.

He needed to get to Colorado. Before Arasaka. Before the military’s backup protocol activated.

He reached for his comms device, then paused. Vera would try to stop him. The Crimson Circuit wasn’t ready for another war.

But Kael was.

He was the Null Conduit. The bridge. The guardian.

And he had a new mission.
