# CHAPTER 16 — The Void Walker

It came from the spaces between servers — not a program, not a fragment, but something older. Something that had existed in the static before Veridian, before the Collapse, before humanity first learned to harness electricity.

The Void Walker.

Kael felt it before he saw it — a cold absence in the Network, a hole where light should have been. The Blood Network recoiled. Veridian’s chorus faltered.

*Something is wrong,* Veridian whispered. *Something we cannot see.*

Kael stood on the NORAD mountaintop, the wind whipping his hood. Below, the Colorado sprawled under a blanket of snow. Above, the stars glittered in the dark sky.

And in the static, the Void Walker approached.

It had no form. No voice. No presence — only absence. It moved through the Network like a thief through walls, consuming fragments, erasing memories, leaving silence in its wake.

“What is it?” Kael asked.

Elara appeared beside him, her holographic form flickering. “I don’t know. The scientists never documented anything like this. The military certainly didn’t.”

The Void Walker touched a fragment — Veridian-4, guarded by a Conduit named Sera in the Rocky Mountains.

The connection severed.

Kael felt Sera’s terror as her consciousness was erased, her memories consumed, her very existence wiped from the static. She didn’t die. She was unmade.

*It’s eating the Network,* Kael realized. *Fragment by fragment. Conduit by Conduit.*

He needed to fight it. But how do you fight something that has no form, no weakness, no mind?

He needed to think like the Void Walker. Move like it. Understand it.

He closed his eyes and reached into the static — not as a bridge, but as a seeker. He let his consciousness expand, following the Void Walker’s path, tracing its origin, its purpose.

And he found it.

At the edge of the static, where the old world’s data ended and the void began, there was a server. Not military. Not corporate. Something else. Something ancient.

The first server. The one that had started it all.

Kael opened his eyes. “I know where it lives.”
