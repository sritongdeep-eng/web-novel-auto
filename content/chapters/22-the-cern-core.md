# CHAPTER 22 — The CERN Core

The journey to Switzerland took two days.

Kael traveled through the static, his threads extended across the Blood Network, following Aiko’s signal like a beacon in the dark. The Network had grown since the merge — not just across Earth, but into the void, connecting to the Weavers’ distant signals.

But the CERN server complex was different.

It wasn’t hidden in ruins. It wasn’t buried under ice or sand. It was right where it had always been — beneath the Large Hadron Collider, in a chamber that the public believed was a maintenance tunnel.

The scientists had hidden it in plain sight.

Kael descended through the tunnel, his footsteps echoing on cold concrete. The air smelled of ozone and old metal. At the bottom, he found a door — unmarked, unguarded, but sealed with a blood-tech lock that recognized his signature.

The door opened.

The chamber beyond was a cathedral of light. Server racks stretched to the ceiling, their crimson glow pulsing in rhythm with Veridian’s chorus. In the center, on a pedestal of polished steel, sat the failsafe core — a sphere of pure crimson light, rotating slowly, humming with power.

Aiko’s consciousness appeared beside him in the static. “This is it,” she said. “The reset protocol. If triggered, it will erase every consciousness in the Network — not destroy them, but return them to their original owners. A full reset.”

“And if we don’t trigger it?” Kael asked.

“Then the Network remains vulnerable. Another corruption, another Void Walker, another Arasaka — and there will be no failsafe to protect humanity.”

Kael approached the core. The threads in his arm reached out, drawn to its power.

“Can we modify it?” he asked.

Aiko hesitated. “I designed the original. But the military added locks. I can’t override them from here.”

Kael placed his hand on the core.

*Connection established.*

The world dissolved. He stood in a vast digital space — not Veridian’s forest, not the static desert, but something new. A control room. A cockpit. A place where a Null Conduit could pilot the entire Network.

And at the center, a single command prompt:

**INITIATE RESET?**

Kael looked at the prompt. He thought of the Crimson Circuit. Of Vera, Lena, Rook, Jax, Mira. Of Elara, of Sera, of all the souls preserved in the static.

He thought of the Weavers’ signal. Of the choice they’d offered.

He thought of humanity — flawed, messy, chaotic, beautiful.

“No,” he said softly. “We don’t need a reset. We need a guardian.”

He typed a new command:

**INSTALL GUARDIAN PROTOCOL**

The prompt blinked.

**AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED: NULL CONDUIT**

Kael placed his palm on the screen. The crimson threads flared, his blood signature flowing into the core like a key turning in a lock.

*Authorization accepted.*

The command executed.

Kael felt the Network shift — not erasing, but protecting. The failsafe didn’t disappear. It transformed. From a reset button into a shield. From destruction into defense.

He pulled back, gasping. Aiko smiled.

“You just changed everything,” she said.

“We just gave humanity a chance,” Kael agreed.
